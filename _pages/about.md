I'm a PhD researcher at the University of Manchester, where I study how the built environment of prisons shapes women's experience of incarceration — and, specifically, its relationship to self-harm. It's a continuation of a question I've been circling since my undergraduate honours thesis on situational crime prevention in Canadian correctional facilities: how much of what happens inside an institution is designed, and how much is simply endured?

Before Manchester, I completed an MRes in Criminological Research at the University of Liverpool (Pass with Distinction), where my dissertation examined rehabilitative architecture across the UK and Canada. My path here wasn't a straight line from an undergraduate degree, though — I've worked in hospitality and professional baking (I hold a Red Seal), spent years in data management, and led a small technology-and-social-impact nonprofit in Calgary before returning fully to research.

I'm the founder of the ECR PRISON network (Early Career Prison Researchers and International Scholars Organised Network), a member of the Carceral Geography Working Group (RGS-IBG), and Department of Criminology TA Rep for 2026–27. My writing — on cult dynamics in social media, Indigenous experiences of parole, and the architecture of rehabilitation — has run in both academic and public-facing venues.

Based in Manchester, UK.
